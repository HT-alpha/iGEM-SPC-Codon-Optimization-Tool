# External comparison, measured on the v2.9.2 tree

This note is a measurement, not a release. The tree is commit
`2a9981a69057486d71b66f8830f64700dcf5b7ec` (changelog title v2.9.2). The package version is
0.1.0. Nothing here is tagged. GATE-BEN-001 is the
existing CAI-only comparison against `cai_greedy` on a different 50-protein
panel. This note does not extend that claim to MFE, to LinearDesign, or to
DNA Chisel.

DNA Design Suite was the release build already installed in `.venv`
(`maturin develop --release`). Wall times are `time.perf_counter` around the
optimiser call on this machine: 4 CPUs, Intel Xeon, 15 GiB RAM, no swap.
They are not a packaging comparison. DNA Chisel's wall time includes starting
a fresh interpreter, because each solve runs in a child process so a stall
can be killed. A Golden Gate solve that both tools finish at CAI 1 still
costs DNA Chisel about half a second of that startup.

  50 nt window between 0.40 and 0.60, BsaI, BsmBI, EcoRI, XbaI, SpeI, PstI
  and NotI on both strands, no homopolymer longer than 6, `AGGAGG` forbidden
  on both strands, no repeated 12-mer on the forward strand (overlaps count,

- Golden Gate: `examples/golden_gate.json`. BsaI and BsmBI, both strands.
- BioBrick RFC[10]: `examples/biobrick_rfc10.json`. EcoRI, XbaI, SpeI, PstI,

DNA Design Suite jobs in this file are budget `standard`, 4 generations, stagnation
window 21, seed 7. The window is longer than 4, so the generation cap is what
stops tier 3. The job inputs that actually ran are tier-1 beam 32, 4 tier-1
seeds, tier-2 block 8, 4 sweeps, population 12. The planning text's beam of
64 is not what this code defaults to.

`cai_greedy` picks the highest relative adaptiveness at each codon and breaks
ties toward the lexicographically smaller codon. It never sees a constraint.
Its wall time was measured once per gene and copied onto each constraint row.
An infeasible CAI of 1 is the unconstrained ceiling, not a feasible design.

## What can be compared

DNA Chisel 3.2.16 was given the same CDS and the same three constraint sets.
`CodonOptimize(method="use_best_codon")` builds `MaximizeCAI`. The codon table
passed in is the ribosomal relative adaptiveness, grouped by amino acid, zeros
left as zeros. Family maxima are 1, so DNA Chisel's score
`log(w) - log(w_max)` equals `log(w)`. A zero is replaced inside DNA Chisel by
`log(0.001)`. The smallest positive ribosomal weight is above 0.001
(`zero_floor_does_not_invert_positive_codons` is true),
so that floor does not reorder the positive codons. DNA Chisel still adds
Met, Trp and the stop into its own objective. The CAI column does not. Both
`random` and `numpy` were seeded. One seed is not the tool; rpsL under the
assembly set was repeated at seeds 1, 7 and 11.

The constraint mapping:

- `EnforceGCContent` steps by 1, the same window starts the DNA Design Suite checker
  uses. The DNA Chisel docstring says `len - window`; the code returns
  `len - window + 1`.
- `AvoidPattern` with no location sets strand 0, which is both strands, so the
  reverse complement is rejected. The enzyme strings match Biopython's sites.
- A homopolymer of 7 or more is `HomopolymerPattern(base, 7)` inside
  `AvoidPattern`. Both strands, so a run of A is also reported as a run of T.
- `UniquifyAllKmers(k=12, include_reverse_complement=False)` matches
  `canonical_strand: false` and overlapping starts. DNA Chisel's own docstring
  says this specification may not solve a k-mer that already occurs more than
  twice. That happened. It is recorded as no solution, not as a CAI.

Three 54 nt inputs were evaluated and not repaired. All three fail both
checkers (`negative_cases_fail_both` is true). They are not pure single-rule
tests: the GC and homopolymer fixtures also trip the 12-mer rule, because a
short repeated sequence contains repeated 12-mers. The BsaI fixture is the
reverse complement `GAGACC`, not the forward site. DNA Design Suite reports
`c_bsai`. DNA Chisel reports `AvoidPattern(pattern:GGTCTC)`. That case is the
evidence the reverse complement is actually enforced.

Of the 59 optimiser sequences that exist (30 DNA Design Suite, 29 DNA Chisel; elastin
assembly has no DNA Chisel sequence), all 59 pass both checkers. There is no
row where one checker accepts and the other rejects.

LinearDesign's objective is folding free energy plus `lambda` times a log
codon weight. The CLI multiplies `lambda` by 100. `lambda` 0 drops the codon
term. Neither value is `usage.cai`, and the binary does not take the
constraint files. Its printed CAI is a log2 geometric mean over every codon,
including Met and Trp, on a frequency table derived from our weights with
zeros floored at 1e-12 (`AGG` and `TAG`). Those printed CAI numbers are not
ours. The table re-scores the mRNA with our CAI and our checker. That is an
evaluation, not a request the tool was asked to satisfy.

CDSfold was not run. `gterai/CDSfold` at `06f3ee8` compiles against ViennaRNA
2.1.1 headers and `libRNA`. This machine has ViennaRNA 2.7.2 as a Python wheel
only: no headers, no `libRNA`. It was not patched onto 2.7.2. There is no
CDSfold number in this study.

## LinearDesign

Not a CAI contest and not a constraint contest. `lambda` 0 and `lambda` 4
were both run. The codon table is the ribosomal weights, zeros floored at
1e-12 and then normalised per amino acid. Verbose was 0, so the binary did
not print its own runtime; `wall s` is ours. The mRNA is 3 nt shorter than
the source CDS wherever the source ended in a stop, because the stop was
removed before the call and LinearDesign does not write one back. The sense
protein matches on every sequence that came back. mRFP has no terminal stop,
so the lengths match.

yagF hit the 120 s timeout at both lambdas. No sequence, no score. lacZ and
yeeJ exited in about 7 s with a non-zero status and no stdout. A separate
rerun of lacZ at `lambda` 0, same binary and same table, was killed by the
OOM killer: the kernel log shows `LinearDesign_2D` at about 14.9 GB RSS on
this 15 GB machine. yeeJ was not rerun. The stored result for both genes is
non-zero and no sequence.

Where a sequence came back, `lambda` 4 raises our CAI and raises their CAI,
and it makes the reported MFE less negative. Several `lambda` 0 sequences
have our CAI exactly 0 because they use a zero-weight codon (`AGG`); their
printed CAI stays positive because of the 1e-12 floor. That is a definition
difference, not a second measurement of the same number. None of the
LinearDesign sequences pass the assembly set. Some pass Golden Gate or
BioBrick and some do not. The tool was not asked to.

## Verdict

DNA Design Suite wins the comparison that is actually the same problem: codon
adaptation on this ribosomal table, same protein, same constraint set.

- Assembly: higher CAI than DNA Chisel on all 9 genes where DNA Chisel
  returns a sequence, and a feasible elastin design where DNA Chisel's 12-mer
  constraint does not solve. Both checkers agree on every returned sequence.
  `cai_greedy` at CAI 1 is infeasible on all 10, so the unconstrained ceiling
  is not a feasible answer. DNA Design Suite does not reach CAI 1 under assembly on
  any of these genes. That gap is the subject of the quality note. It is not
  a loss to DNA Chisel.
- Golden Gate and BioBrick: tie at the exact pattern-DP optimum on most genes
  of at most 2500 nt. Where the strings differ, DNA Design Suite's CAI is higher
  and still feasible. DNA Chisel is never higher.
- DNA Chisel is seed-sensitive on rpsL assembly (three seeds, three sequences,
  CAI from 0.95775539455032 to 0.9641706286088719). DNA Design Suite's rpsL assembly result does not move
  across seeds 1, 2, 7, 11 and 42.
- LinearDesign is a different objective. Report its MFE and its own CAI as
  its numbers. Do not say DNA Design Suite matches them, and do not say GATE-BEN-001
  covers them. On this machine it does not finish yagF within 120 s, and lacZ
  allocates about 15 GB and is killed.
- CDSfold was not installed. No number.
- Accessibility in the tables is a post-hoc fold. Neither constrained
  optimiser was asked to optimise it here. It is not a winner.

What this does not show: a global MFE comparison, a claim that the assembly
CAI is optimal, or a speed ranking. DNA Chisel's wall times include process
startup. On the long assembly genes its solve-plus-startup is still in the
same few seconds as DNA Design Suite, and its CAI is worse. Speed is out of scope
for this note.

The loss to write down is inside our own search, not against DNA Chisel.
Under assembly, tier 1 cannot carry GC windows or scattered 12-mers
(`CF-SRCH-017`). The design is whatever deterministic seed repair plus a
small NSGA population can do from there. The quality note measures how far
that is from an optimum.
