# Generality corpus

DNA Design Suite, package version 0.1.0. The before column is commit
`0e70d4b` on the same machine and the same gene list. Nothing here is tagged.

The question was whether codon design holds on a broad set of real genes,
or only on the few sequences the pins name. The sample is 255 coding
sequences from the shipped *Escherichia coli* K-12 MG1655 file
`benchmarks/corpus/NC_000913.3_cds.fna.gz` (accession NC_000913.3). The draw
is seed 20261018, stratified by length bin and GC tertile, plus the records
richest in rare codons under the ribosomal weight table. The list is
`benchmarks/corpus/generality_sample.json`. Its digest is
`f1cf5a7a172bdf4974d942fe7a2e00aaf069eb5f60f627d92b1028b211b214dd`.

Length bins are 0–300, 300–900, 900–1800, and 1800 nt and above. A counted
codon is rare for the draw when its weight is at or below the 10th percentile
of the positive weights in `examples/mg1655_ribosomal_usage.json`. That
cutoff is the table's own percentile.

Each gene ran under every budget and constraint preset below. Host
`escherichia_coli:511145/MG1655`, translation table 11, seed 7, stagnation
window 8. The second usage table is `examples/kazusa_e_coli_316407.json`
(Kazusa *E. coli* O157:H7, 316407). That is 8 runs per gene, 2040 runs.

The assembly file is `benchmarks/validation/constraints_assembly.json`.
CAI in the tables is `benchmarks/validation/cai_independent.py`. The
constraint-free optimum for a gene and a usage table is that gene's empty
preset on the same table.

Reproduce from the repository root after `make build`:

```sh
.venv/bin/python tools/generality_corpus.py sample
.venv/bin/python tools/generality_corpus.py run --out /tmp/generality
.venv/bin/python tools/generality_corpus.py summarize /tmp/generality/rows.jsonl
.venv/bin/python tools/generality_corpus.py compare before.jsonl after.jsonl
```

`sample` rewrites the gene list only when you ask it to. `run` reloads the
checked-in list and checks its digest against the gzip.

## What the 2040 runs did

Every run succeeded. The designed protein matches the input. No run returned
`CF-SRCH-006`. No run timed out. No run reported an actionable constraint
violation. The same is true of the before column and the after column.

Seconds per kilobase stay flat as genes get longer, and on several presets
they fall. A fixed startup cost makes short genes look more expensive per
kilobase. The long tail is the longest genes, in seconds, growing about with
length. The sample does not show superlinear scaling.

### CAI

With no hard constraints, the design matches the empty-preset optimum on
every gene (median gap 0, minimum gap 0). Twenty-two ribosomal designs sit
below `cai_greedy`, and seven Kazusa designs do. All twenty-two ribosomal
cases start with GTG (15) or TTG (7). The largest gap is ymiA, 165 nt, TTG,
CAI 0.939590. The start codon is locked, and the greedy sequence is free to
open with ATG. The gap to the constraint-free optimum is 0. That is the
weight table applied to a locked start, and it is left as it is.

Under assembly rules the median CAI sits 0.022 below the ribosomal optimum
and 0.043 below the Kazusa optimum. The wide gaps are short genes, and they
are feasible:

`assembly_standard` runs 12 generations. All 255 stopped at generation 9 on
hypervolume stagnation, and the CAI figures above are unchanged from the
fast assembly run. The gap is the cost of a feasible sequence under those
rules. Extra generations on a feasible seed do not buy it back. The same
pattern holds for harsh: 241 of 255 ribosomal designs sit below `cai_greedy`,
all of them feasible, minimum gap −0.270 on mgtL.

Structure uses the assembly rules plus a windowed accessibility term. The
median CAI gap to the optimum is −0.026. The widest is rzoR again, −0.212.
All 255 structure runs stopped at the generation limit of 2.

CAI moved on one row. yjbD, 273 nt, Kazusa weights, assembly rules, rose
from 0.932524 to 0.941316 (delta +0.008792). The design is feasible and the
protein matches. The other 2039 coding-sequence hashes match the before
column, including every structure objective vector. No gene lost CAI.
`assembly_standard` still stops at generation 9 on hypervolume stagnation,
all 255 genes. Structure still stops at generation 2, all 255.

The corpus did not contain a stalled job, a `CF-SRCH-006`, or a timeout to
repair. The changes below are general, and the rerun is how they were judged.

**Escape when a seed is still infeasible** (`DEP-SRCH-024`). After the
existing middle-of-window GC pairs, a seed that is still infeasible tries at
most four pair edits. The sites are a spread of up to eight codons across the
whole stuck GC window, edges included, and up to eight codons on over-limit
k-mers, capped at twelve sites. A pair is kept only when the packed progress
score falls and pattern hits do not rise. A feasible seed never takes the
branch, which is why the short-gene assembly gaps above are unchanged. yjbD
under the Kazusa table is the one corpus row this branch improved. The
published assembly pins (waaS, yeeJ, rpoC, and the seed-assembly fixtures)
match the previous sequences.

**Stop when the front stops changing** (`DEP-SRCH-025`). Tier 3 also stops
when the non-dominated front's fingerprint is unchanged for the caller's
stagnation window. Hypervolume is checked first, so a run that has both
signals still reports hypervolume stagnation. On this sample the standard
presets already stop that way at generation 9, and the fingerprint stop does
not move them. The fingerprint is FNV-1a and is stored in the tier-3
checkpoint. With a window of 8, the stop needs nine identical fronts, so a
run of four generations or fewer cannot trip it.

**Wall and memory caps** (`DEP-BUDG-002`). A job enforces the published wall
for its tier, scaled by CDS length between the 2 kb and 15 kb anchors, and
an 8 GiB resident cap. At or under 2 kb the fast tier is 30 s and the
standard tier is 600 s. At 15 kb those are 120 s and 3600 s. Past 15 kb the
same line continues. A deadline is checked on every call. The resident
reading may be reused for 200 ms. Passing either cap returns `CF-BUDGET-003`
and the detail names the wall or the memory cap. No corpus job hit the cap.
The longest after-run was 6.46 s. Callers that omit the fields stay unlimited.

**Repair and structure cost.** A tier-3 repair probe now takes the violation
status and the hit count from one constraint check. Windowed accessibility
folds a cluster of codon edits once when their fold regions overlap or abut,
and folds distant edits separately. Folding the span between distant edits
would recompute bases neither edit can change. The penalty matches a full
fold of the edited sequence. Full folds on the structure preset stayed 442
before and after. The yeeJ structure pin keeps the objective vector
`[0.9723247970586831, 2.4166863375261896]`, 12 hits, and 1 full fold. The
local-edit counter on that pin is 11, down from 18, because an incremental
evaluation is counted once. Across the 255 structure genes the median
local-edit count moved from 14 to 10 for the same reason. The wall-time
column is the cost that changed: structure's paired median ratio is 0.947,
and the standard assembly preset, where tier 3 actually repairs, is 0.919.
